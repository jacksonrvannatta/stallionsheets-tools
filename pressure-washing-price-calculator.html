How pressure washing jobs are usually priced

Many pressure washing businesses quote by the square foot, because area is easy to measure and easy for a customer to understand. Others charge by the hour or by the job. This calculator uses the square foot method and adds extras and a minimum charge.

Pick a rate you can defend

Your per-square-foot rate has to cover your time, fuel, water, chemicals, equipment wear, insurance and taxes. If you know what you need to earn per hour and how many square feet you wash in an hour, divide one by the other for a rate based on your own numbers. The hourly rate calculator can help with the first number.

Add-ons and extras

Sealing, stain treatment, gutter work, hard-to-reach areas and travel can each be priced as a flat add-on. Enter up to three here.

Why a minimum charge matters

A small job still costs you travel, setup and cleanup. A minimum job charge keeps a 100 square foot walkway from costing you money. The calculator applies your minimum whenever the total comes in below it.

Measuring the area

For a flat surface, multiply length by width. For a house, measure the wall length and height. Walk the site first, since steep slopes, heavy staining and long hoses all add time.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a pressure washing price?

Multiply the square feet by your rate per square foot, add any extras, and make sure the total meets your minimum job charge.

What rate per square foot should I use?

Use a rate based on your own costs and speed, and compare it with local prices. This tool does not set a market rate for you.

Should I charge a minimum?

Many businesses do, so small jobs still cover travel and setup. The amount is up to you.

Is this a guaranteed price?

No. It is an estimate. Inspect the site and adjust for conditions before you quote.
