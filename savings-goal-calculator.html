How much should you save each month?

A savings goal feels much more doable when it is broken into small regular amounts. Enter how much you want to save, how much you already have, and the date you need it. The calculator tells you the amount to set aside each week or each month to get there.

How the calculation works

It subtracts what you have already saved from your goal, then divides the rest by the number of whole weeks or months until your date. If you enter an interest rate, it accounts for the interest your savings would earn at a steady rate, so the required deposit is a little lower. If you leave the rate at zero, it is plain division.

Choose either the weekly amount or the monthly amount, not both. Weekly is handy if you are paid weekly or every two weeks, because small amounts are easier to move than a large monthly lump.

Making the goal realistic

If the number looks too high, you have three levers: move the date later, lower the goal, or raise what you have already saved with a one-time deposit. Try each in the calculator and see which feels right. A smaller goal you reach builds momentum.

Make the saving automatic. Set up a recurring transfer to a separate savings account on payday, so the money moves before you can spend it. Keeping it in its own account, ideally labeled with the goal, makes it harder to dip into.

Common goals

Popular targets include an emergency fund, a trip, a down payment, a car repair fund, holiday spending and annual bills such as insurance or taxes. For an emergency fund, many people aim for a few months of essential expenses, but the right size depends on your situation.

What to remember

Interest rates change, and accounts may charge fees, so treat the result as a planning estimate. Tracking each deposit in a budget, so you can see progress toward the goal, is one of the best ways to stay motivated.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate how much to save each month?

Subtract what you have saved from your goal and divide by the number of months until your deadline. This calculator does that for you and also shows the weekly amount.

Does the calculator include interest?

Only if you enter a yearly interest rate. With a rate of 0, it is simple division.

Should I save weekly or monthly?

Whichever matches how you are paid. Use one plan or the other, not both.

What if my target date is very soon?

The required amount will be large. Try a later date or a smaller goal to see what is realistic.

Is my data stored?

No. The math runs in your browser and nothing is sent or saved.
