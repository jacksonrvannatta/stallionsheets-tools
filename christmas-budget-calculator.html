How to set a Christmas budget that works

Holiday spending creeps up because it happens in small pieces: a gift here, a sale there, shipping, wrapping paper, a party dish, a card. The easiest way to stay in control is to decide the total first, then divide it before you shop.

Start with a number you can pay for without borrowing. Look at your income between now and the holidays and decide what you can set aside. Then enter that total here, and the calculator divides it across your list.

Even split or by priority

An even split is the simplest: everyone gets the same amount. It works well for a list of similar relationships, such as a set of cousins.

The priority option lets you give more to the people closest to you. Give each person a number from 1 to 5, and the budget is divided in proportion. Someone with a 4 receives twice as much as someone with a 2. Adjust the numbers until the amounts look right to you.

Why hold back a percentage

Gift prices are only part of the cost. Wrapping, gift bags, shipping, batteries, stocking stuffers and last-minute extras add up. Holding back around 10 percent from the start keeps those costs from pushing you over. If you do not need that cushion, set it to zero.

Tips to stay on budget

Write the list down with a dollar limit beside every name. Shop with the list, not the sale. Keep a running total as you buy, whether in a notebook or a spreadsheet, so you always know what is left. Consider homemade gifts, shared family gifts, or a name draw for large groups, which means each person buys for one other person instead of everyone.

Do not forget costs outside gifts: travel, food, decorations and events. If those matter in your household, include them as people on the list or subtract them from the total first.

After the holidays

Compare what you planned with what you spent. Notes from this year make next year easier, and starting a small monthly holiday fund in January keeps the next December from being a shock.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I spend on Christmas?

There is no right amount. Pick a total you can pay for from money you already have, without borrowing.

What does the priority number do?

Each person receives a share of the gift budget in proportion to their priority. A 4 gets twice as much as a 2.

Why is there a hold-back percentage?

To cover wrapping, shipping and extras so they do not break the budget. Set it to 0 if you do not want it.

Does this work for Hanukkah, birthdays or other occasions?

Yes. It simply splits any budget across a list of people.

Is my list saved?

No. Everything stays in your browser and disappears when you close the page.
