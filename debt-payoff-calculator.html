How this debt payoff calculator works

Enter each debt with its balance, its APR (the yearly interest rate on your statement) and its minimum monthly payment. Then add any extra money you can put toward debt each month. The calculator runs your payments month by month, adds interest to each balance, and tells you the month the last balance hits zero and how much interest you paid along the way.

Your total monthly payment stays the same the whole time. When one debt is paid off, the payment that went to it rolls into the next debt. This rolling payment is what makes both methods work.

Snowball vs avalanche

With the avalanche method you put every extra dollar on the debt with the highest interest rate first. This usually costs the least in interest, because the most expensive debt shrinks first.

With the snowball method you put extra dollars on the smallest balance first, whatever the rate. You may pay a little more interest, but small debts disappear faster, and some people find the quick wins help them stick with the plan. The calculator shows both results side by side so you can see the actual difference for your numbers.

The best method is the one you will actually keep doing. If the two results are close, pick the one that motivates you.

Tips for paying debt off faster

Even a small extra payment shortens the timeline. Try changing the extra amount above and watch the date move. Use windfalls such as a tax refund or a bonus as one-time lump sums. Stop adding new charges to the cards you are paying down, because new spending undoes the progress.

Call your card company and ask whether a lower rate is available, and check whether a balance transfer or consolidation loan has fees that cancel out the savings. A written monthly budget makes it much easier to find the extra money in the first place.

What this calculator does not include

It assumes fixed rates and no new purchases. Variable-rate cards, promotional 0% periods, late fees and annual fees are not modeled. Use it as a clear estimate for planning, then check against your real statements.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between the snowball and avalanche methods?

Snowball pays the smallest balance first for quick wins. Avalanche pays the highest interest rate first, which usually costs the least interest overall.

Does the calculator include my minimum payments?

Yes. It pays every minimum each month and puts your extra payment on the target debt. When a debt is paid off, its payment rolls to the next one.

What if my payments do not cover the interest?

Then the balance never reaches zero. The calculator will tell you so. Raise your minimums or extra payment to make progress.

Is my information saved or sent anywhere?

No. The math runs in your browser and nothing you type is sent or stored.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is an estimate for planning. Check numbers against your lender statements.
