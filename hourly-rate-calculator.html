How to figure out what to charge per hour

Most people pick an hourly rate by looking at what others charge, or by taking a wage they once earned and adding a little. That often leaves a self-employed person underpaid, because a business owner pays costs an employee does not: taxes, supplies, fuel, insurance, software and unpaid time.

This calculator works backward from the income you want. It takes your target take-home pay, grosses it up for taxes, adds your business expenses, and divides the total by the hours you can realistically bill in a year.

Why billable hours matter

Billable hours are the hours a customer pays for. If you work 40 hours a week but spend 15 of them driving, quoting, answering messages and doing paperwork, you only have about 25 billable hours. Dividing by 40 instead of 25 gives a rate that looks fine on paper but leaves you short every month. Track a typical week before you trust your number.

Weeks off count too. Holidays, sick days, slow seasons and vacation do not pay. Subtracting them gives an honest count of the hours you can bill.

What to include in business expenses

Add up the monthly costs of running the work: vehicle and fuel, tools and supplies, cleaning products, insurance, phone, software, advertising and licenses. If a cost is yearly, divide by 12. Leave out your personal living costs, because those come out of your take-home pay.

Taxes and your rate

Self-employed people usually owe both income tax and self-employment tax. The percentage varies by location and income, so enter the rate you or your tax preparer expects and set that money aside each time you are paid. A local tax professional can give you the right figure.

Using the result

Treat the result as your break-even floor, not a price to advertise. Many contractors quote jobs by the project and use the hourly figure to check that the project price covers their time. If your local market will not pay your number, adjust the inputs: more billable hours, lower expenses or a lower income goal. Tracking jobs, income and expenses in one place makes this much easier.

Frequently asked questions

What is a billable hour?

An hour you can charge a customer for. Travel, quoting, admin and marketing time are usually not billable.

Why is my hourly rate higher than a regular wage?

Because it has to cover taxes, business expenses, and the unpaid hours and weeks you do not bill.

What tax percentage should I use?

It depends on where you live and how much you earn. Ask a tax professional, or use a cautious estimate and adjust later.

Should I charge by the hour or by the job?

Either can work. Many service businesses quote by the job and use this hourly figure to check that the price covers their time.

Does this guarantee I will earn this much?

No. It shows the rate you need to reach your target if you bill the hours you entered. Actual income depends on finding and completing the work.
