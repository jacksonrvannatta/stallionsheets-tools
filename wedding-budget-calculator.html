How to split a wedding budget

Start with the total you can spend, then decide how much each category gets. Many couples begin with a common set of percentages and adjust from there. The starting split here is a guideline for planning, not a fact or a rule.

Where the biggest share usually goes

The venue and food are often the largest part of a budget, since they scale with the number of guests. Photography, music, flowers and attire follow. A buffer for surprises is worth keeping, because costs often change between the first quote and the final bill.

Make it yours

If music matters more to you than flowers, raise one and lower the other. The calculator updates the dollar amounts and tells you if your percentages no longer add up to 100.

Guest count changes everything

Cost per guest is shown when you enter a guest count. Cutting the list is one of the most direct ways to lower the total, since food, drink and space are charged per person.

After the split

Record estimates and actual payments as they happen, so you can see what is left in each category. Quotes in your area may be very different from these percentages, so compare with real quotes before you commit.

Frequently asked questions

What percentage of a wedding budget goes to the venue?

Many planning guides suggest venue and catering together take a large share. This calculator starts at 45% as a common guideline, and you can change it.

Are these percentages accurate for my wedding?

They are common guidelines, not facts. Costs differ by location, season and priorities, so edit them.

Should I keep a buffer?

Many couples do, because costs often rise. The calculator starts with 9% as a guideline.

Does this include a honeymoon?

No. Add it to your total or plan it separately.
